## The question clients keep asking us

Every few weeks, a client conversation lands on the same three questions:

**Which consulting firm should we pick for AI work? How do we even choose? And is any of it actually working?**

The honest answer used to be: it depends, and most of what you can read is marketing. Firms publish ambitious AI narratives, announce partnerships, reorganize practice lines — but there was no systematic way to tell signal from noise, or to see who is actually doing the work versus who is just talking about it.

So we stopped arguing from opinions and started collecting evidence.

## What we built

For the past **20 weeks**, we have tracked **17 traditional consulting and professional services firms** — MBB, the Big Four, Accenture, Capgemini, and the specialist and boutique strategy houses. Every week we sweep official newsrooms, partner ecosystems, trade media and press wires, and log each firm's public AI actions into a structured evidence base. **277 events** so far.

Each event is an entry with a date, a type, a summary, a confidence level, and — critically — **a link to the original source**, so every claim can be checked.

We then map the evidence onto nine weighted dimensions: strategy signal, depth of technology partnerships, proprietary platforms and assets, delivery and production capability, M&A and capital moves, industry coverage, client value proof, AI governance, and thought leadership. Recent evidence counts more than old evidence, so **standing still on this board means slowly losing ground**.

## This week on the board

Issue 20 was one of the busiest weeks we have tracked:

- **Oliver Wyman agreed to acquire Synpulse**, a 1,200-person financial-services technology and AI engineering consultancy — the heaviest capital move of the week, and a clear bet that financial-services AI delivery is bought, not built.
- **KPMG had a platform week**: the US launch of KPMG Clara intelligence (AI agents embedded in audit workflows, with a planned rollout to all private-company audits in 2027), plus an FT-reported pilot that ran **100+ AI agents in a parallel audit of a FTSE 250 company**.
- **McKinsey formed an alliance with Cognition** to bring Devin, the autonomous AI software engineer, into enterprise delivery.
- **EY shipped two organizational moves**: an Agentic Cyber Defense Center built with Microsoft, and the introduction of Forward Deployed Engineers into its US assurance practice.

The pattern behind these headlines is consistent: **firms are no longer announcing AI strategies — they are buying capabilities, embedding engineers, and putting agents into production workflows.**

## The part we did not expect: the board converging

When we started, the board was noisy. In week 2, the average firm moved **2.5 rank positions** in a single week, and 12 of 17 firms changed places. Early rankings were thin on evidence, so a single announcement could reshuffle the table.

Twenty weeks and 277 events later, the board has settled. Recent weeks show an average swing of about **0.5 positions**, with 4–6 movers instead of 12–15. BCG has held the #1 spot since September. Deloitte, McKinsey and Accenture have separated clearly from the pack — and this week's moves are driven by substantial evidence, not headline count.

We think that convergence is the real product. **A ranking that keeps reshuffling every week is unusable for decisions.** One that moves only when meaningful evidence arrives starts to answer the client's question: the differences between firms are now stable enough to act on, and the direction of travel is visible.

## How to read it — and how not to

Three honest caveats:

1. **This is a research preview, not an authoritative ranking.** The index measures recent public actions, weighted by evidence quality — not overall capability or client outcomes.
2. **It only sees what firms publish.** Quiet firms are not necessarily doing nothing; they just added nothing publicly verifiable this week.
3. **We deliberately exclude negative signals** (disputes, lawsuits, quality incidents). The board measures forward action; the market already prices the rest.
